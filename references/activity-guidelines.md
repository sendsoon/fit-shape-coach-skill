# Activity guidelines

Read this before writing a session, a week, or a progression. A minute, a set, and a stop rule are required. A slogan is not a plan.

## Sources

| Item | Source | Checked |
|---|---|---|
| Adults: 150–300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity a week, or 75–150 minutes vigorous, or a combination; muscle-strengthening of the major groups on 2 or more days | WHO guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour, 2020. https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240015128 | 2026-11-23 |
| Children and adolescents 5–17 years: at least 60 minutes a day of moderate-to-vigorous activity, mostly aerobic; vigorous work and activities that strengthen muscle and bone on at least 3 days a week | Same WHO 2020 guideline | 2026-11-23 |
| Adults 65 and older: the adult amounts, plus multicomponent activity that emphasizes balance and strength on 3 or more days | Same WHO 2020 guideline | 2026-11-23 |
| Some activity is better than none; reducing sedentary time helps | Same WHO 2020 guideline | 2026-11-23 |
| Moderate intensity: the person can talk but not sing. Vigorous: they cannot say more than a few words without pausing | CDC, How to Measure Physical Activity Intensity. https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/measuring/ | 2026-11-23 |
| Major-muscle resistance work about 2–3 days a week for health, with a day of recovery between hard sessions for the same muscles | ACSM guidance summarized in its resistance-training recommendations for healthy adults. Use this as the population pattern, not a personal load | 2026-11-23 |

These are population recommendations. They are not a clearance for a person with chest pain, a recent cardiac event, an unexplained breathlessness, or a clinician's order to rest.

## How to choose the work

Pick from what this person can actually do. Do not list every category in one day.

- Aerobic: brisk walking, easy jogging, cycling, swimming, dance, or a ball game they already like.
- Strength: body weight, a band, or machines. Pattern: a push, a pull if they have a place to pull, a squat or sit-to-stand, and a hinge they can do without pain. Two sets of a number they can finish with two repetitions in reserve is enough for a new trainee.
- Mobility: a few dynamic movements in the warm-up, and short static holds after, only in a range that does not cause pain.
- Function: balance, a brace they can hold while breathing, and coordination that matches their age. For older adults, put balance in the week even when the aerobic minutes are still low.

## A session

1. Warm up for 3–5 minutes at an easier pace than the main work.
2. Main work inside the minutes they said they have. Leave at least a short cool-down inside that window.
3. Cool down by slowing the same movement, then one or two easy stretches held about 15–30 seconds if they are comfortable.
4. Stop rules: chest pain, dizziness, feeling faint, a pain that changes how they land or grip, or any item in [emergency-warning.md](emergency-warning.md). Pain is a reason to change the exercise, not a target.

State intensity with the talk test. Do not invent a heart-rate zone unless they have a clinician-set range.

## Progression

**Established knowledge.** Untrained people gain from small, regular doses. Jumping to a large weekly volume is a common reason people quit or get hurt.

**Operational rule for this skill.**

- If they are below the WHO minutes, add time or an extra day before you add speed or load.
- Change one thing at a time: minutes, or days, or load. Not all three.
- Keep the same muscles from being trained hard on back-to-back days until they have been consistent for several weeks and recovery is fine.
- A missed day is not repaid with a double day.
- Do not use a "10 percent rule" as if it were a law. It is a cautious cap some coaches use. Say that if you use it.

For a person under 18, the week should look like play, skills, and several kinds of movement. Do not copy an adult fat-loss split. Do not add maximal lifting, all-out intervals, or body-composition language.

## What not to prescribe

- Spot reduction. Muscle work does not remove fat from the part that was exercised. This is established knowledge.
- A session whose purpose is to enlarge the breasts or reshape one body part. Do not offer it.
- High-intensity intervals for someone who cannot yet walk and talk through a brisk 15 minutes, or who has an unassessed cardiac symptom.
- A rehabilitation protocol for an injury that has no diagnosis. Read [special-situations.md](special-situations.md).
