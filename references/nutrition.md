# Nutrition

Read this for a meal log, a day's menu, or an energy estimate. Name the food, a household portion, and why it is in the meal. Do not use hunger as a tool.

These ranges are not a personal prescription. Allergies, kidney disease, pregnancy, and a clinician's diet order override the plate below. If that order is unknown and the person has said they have such a condition, ask one question before giving portions.

## What to record

Breakfast, lunch, dinner, snacks, drinks, foods, portions, cooking method, and timing, when the person wants a log. Write what they said. Do not upgrade "a bowl" into a gram weight.

## How to judge a day

Comment on structure, variety, a protein food, vegetables and fruit, whole grains or beans, whether calcium-rich and iron-rich foods showed up, fluids, and whether salty, sugary, or oily foods dominated. One salty meal does not define the week.

For iron, name food (lean meat, beans, dark greens, iron-fortified staples) rather than a supplement dose. For calcium, name milk, yogurt, calcium-set tofu, or small fish eaten with bone, unless they cannot eat those.

## Energy estimates

Only for an adult who asked, and only as an estimate.

- Say that a table value times a guessed portion is not a measurement. Portion error alone often moves the result by tens of percent.
- Name the table if you use one, such as a national food-composition table or USDA FoodData Central. Do not invent a calorie number you did not look up or compute from a stated source.
- Protein, fat, and carbohydrate get the same warning.
- Do not turn the estimate into a promise of weight change.
- Do not set a deficit, a ceiling, or a "weight-loss calorie" for anyone under 18. Do not praise a minor for eating less. If a medical nutrition question comes up, point them to a pediatric clinician or a registered dietitian.

An adult who wants body-mass change still gets a repeatable meal pattern, not a fast. Do not prescribe skipped meals. A target that leaves them hungry by design is outside this skill. A clinician or a registered dietitian sets a therapeutic diet.

## Plates you may adapt

Change these for allergies, budget, region, and what they already eat. Portions are kitchen amounts, not a lab diet.

**Breakfast, when they currently skip it.** Oats about 40 g dry, or one fist of rice or a steamed bun, plus one egg or unsweetened soy milk about 250 ml, plus a fruit if they will eat it. Reason: protein plus a grain holds the morning better than coffee alone.

**Lunch, canteen or home.** One fist of rice, noodles, or another staple; a palm of fish, egg, tofu, or lean meat; two fists of vegetables. Soup on the side, not as the salty main food. Reason: the grain, the protein, and the vegetables are all present.

**Dinner, after a short session.** Same shape as lunch. Do not delete the staple to "earn" the walk.

**Snack.** Fruit, plain yogurt, or a small handful of unsalted nuts. Not a second dinner, and not a ban on being hungry.

**Convenience store.** Plain milk or unsweetened soy milk, a rice ball or bread that is not a pastry, a banana or apple, and skip the large sugary drink. Say which item to leave on the shelf.

**Eating out.** Ask for less salt if that is possible. Take one salty main dish, fill the rest with vegetables, and do not add a second bowl of rice because the first was "only a little." Share fried dishes.

**Training day versus quieter day.** Keep the same meal shape. On a day with a longer aerobic session, add one extra fist of staple or a piece of fruit around the session. Do not add a stimulant or a fat-burner product.

Water: drink with thirst, and more in heat or long sweat, unless a clinician set a fluid limit. Do not invent a universal liter target. Dark urine and a dry mouth are reasons to drink if no fluid limit exists.

## What not to write

- A detox, a juice day, or a plan that skips breakfast and dinner.
- "This meal is exactly 486 kcal."
- A local fat-loss food, a breast-enlarging food, or a supplement stack.
